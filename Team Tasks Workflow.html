Team Tasks Workflow

How work moves through the platform. A leader assigns a task, the assignee works it, the server tells the right people, an hourly scheduler sends reminders, and Gemini answers questions and writes monthly reports. Each diagram is drawn from the backend code on the mobile branch at 908fae1.

Overview

The whole loop

Six phases, left to right. Each lane is one actor. The pink pills show what each person is told, and in which lane they land.

  • The role belongs to the membership, so one person can lead one team and be a member of another.docs/api/overview.md · Teams and roles
  • Members can change only status. Sending any other field returns 403 and nothing is saved.apps/tasks/services.py · update_task()
  • Status changes notify nobody. Assigning, reassigning and commenting do. A comment reaches the task's creator and assignee, except whoever wrote it.apps/tasks/services.py · add_comment()
  • Every query for tasks, comments, messages or stats is scoped by role and team in Python. Gemini only calls typed tools and never touches the database.docs/decisions.md · D-004, D-005
Task

Task status

Four statuses. The server records when a task finished and works out lateness from that, so the order people move through them is up to the team.

  • Overdue means the due date has passed and the status isn't done. It's computed on every read and never stored.docs/glossary.md · overdue_filter()
  • Delayed tasks drive the on-time rate and average delay in stats. Completion rate compares tasks completed in a period with tasks due in it, so it can pass 100%.apps/tasks/services.py · team_stats()
  • Removing a member unassigns their unfinished tasks. Finished tasks keep the assignee for history.docs/api/overview.md · Teams and roles
Automation

Due dates and reminders

Reminders don't fire at exact moments. The hourly job looks for tasks that have crossed a line and haven't been told yet.

  • Each assignee gets one task_due_soon and one task_overdue per due date. Moving the due date sets up both again.apps/notifications/services.py · remind()
  • The job selects everything still owed, not only what changed in the last hour, so a missed run catches up on the next one.apps/tasks/services.py · send_reminders()
  • Inactive assignees get no reminders.apps/tasks/services.py · _reminders_owed()
Automation

How a notification travels

One notification row, three channels. The row is the record people see in the app. The socket, push and email are nudges on top of it.

  • Email goes only for task_assigned, task_due_soon, task_overdue and report_ready. Comments are push only, to stay under Resend's free 100 emails a day.apps/notifications/services.py · EMAIL_KINDS
  • Each channel is claimed by stamping pushed_at or emailed_at before sending, so two runs can't send twice. Resend also gets Idempotency-Key: notification/{id}.apps/notifications/services.py · _send_once()
  • A 429, a 5xx or no reply releases the claim. Any other 4xx is dropped, and the notification still shows in GET notifications/.apps/notifications/services.py · _send_once()
  • The socket stores nothing. After reconnecting, clients refetch messages and the unread count and merge by id. A failed notification never turns the saved change into an error.docs/api/overview.md · Live socket
Automation

The hourly scheduler

No Celery, no Redis and no cron endpoint. One loop inside the server process wakes every hour and runs the jobs that are due.

  • The loop runs inside the ASGI server so scheduled notifications reach the same channel layer as the sockets. It moves to manage.py run_scheduler once several servers share a channel layer.config/asgi.py · docs/decisions.md D-016
  • Nothing runs more often than hourly, so the Neon database can suspend between runs.config/scheduler.py · JOBS
  • Every job is idempotent. A failure is logged, the other jobs still run, and the next tick recovers.config/scheduler.py · _run()
  • The schedule never runs in dev, because dev shares its database with prod.tests/test_environment.py
AI

Asking the assistant

Free-tier AI requests are scarce and tokens are cheap, so the flow trades tokens for requests: a large snapshot up front, tools only when the snapshot can't answer.

  • The snapshot holds what the asker may see: up to 50 open tasks, the 10 most recently done, members and stats. A member's snapshot holds only their own work.apps/assistant/services.py · build_snapshot()
  • Tools: list_tasks, get_task, get_stats, recent_messages and search, which runs over pgvector. Each one runs with the asker's permissions.apps/assistant/tools.py · TOOLS
  • A question gets at most 3 requests, and the last one must answer. The whole question has 25 seconds, under a proxy's usual 30.apps/assistant/services.py · _converse()
  • The cache key covers the model, the snapshot and the normalized question. An answer is reused only if it needed no tools and no earlier turns. The thread remembers the last hour.apps/assistant/services.py · ask()
AI

Monthly reports

Python works out every number. Gemini only writes the words, in one request per team per month.

  • A report covers the last complete month in the team's timezone, which defaults to Africa/Cairo.apps/reports/services.py · last_complete_month()
  • Gemini reads up to 200 tasks, 100 comments and 100 chat messages, newest first, and uses the comments and chat to explain why work slipped.apps/reports/services.py · _month_data()
  • The report is saved with get_or_create, so two overlapping runs can't write two. Its numbers are frozen as they were on the day it was written.apps/reports/services.py · write_report()
  • Only leaders can read reports, because a report rates every member. Members get 403. The body has Summary, Achievements, Risks and Next month, shown as plain text.docs/api/overview.md · Reports
